# Legitimacy and transparency checklist

Work through these. Record each as: confirmed, not found, or concern. "Not found" is not the same as "concern".

## Company
- Does the company have an official site, press coverage, funding or registration records, and a consistent presence across sources?
- Is its size and stage plausible for the role (for example, a "founding" or "head of" title at a long-established company needs an explanation)?
- Does the role match what the company actually does?

## Poster or recruiter
- Is the person's profile consistent with the company (title, tenure, connections, history)?
- Is the poster the hiring manager, an in-house recruiter, or an agency or third party? Agencies are normal but should be named as such.
- Are the company's official channels (careers page, company page) listing the same role?
- A company email domain is a good sign. A personal email domain on its own is weak evidence, not proof of a problem.

## The post itself
Common in real roles but worth noting as "thin": no location, work mode, pay, JD link, team name, or application process; comment-bait mechanics with large comment counts.

## Scam or low-quality patterns (treat as serious)
- Any request for money (training, "registration", equipment), or for sensitive documents (ID numbers, bank details) before an offer
- Interviews only over chat apps, with immediate offers
- Pay that is far above market for the level, or vague "work from home, earn daily"
- No identifiable employer
- Pressure to act urgently or to keep the opportunity private
- Messages from lookalike domains or profiles with very new accounts and stock photos

## Posting freshness (is the role still open?)
A post can come from a real company and still be a poor use of the seeker's time. Three different cases: **fake** (a scam, covered above), **stale** (filled or closed but never removed), and **ghost** (a real company with no active hire, for example pipeline building, a hiring freeze, or an evergreen role). Ghost and stale posts are not necessarily dishonest, so describe them neutrally.

Check, using only what the post shows and what search finds:
- Post date, and any "reposted" label. A role open for months, or repeatedly reposted, is a warning sign.
- Whether the same title (ideally with a requisition ID) is on the company's own careers page.
- Whether the post still accepts applications (a "no longer accepting" label, a broken or closed apply form).
- Recent evidence of hiring: people who recently joined in this role on the company's page, and news of layoffs or a hiring freeze.
- Duplicates: the same distinctive text on many boards over many months suggests a recycled or evergreen post.
- Older versions of the careers page (Wayback Machine) to see how long the role has been listed.

Many posts show no date. In that case say the freshness is **unclear**; do not call it stale. Never state how common ghost jobs are unless you found a current source.

## How to report
Give a one-line legitimacy read (Confirmed company and plausible post / Real company, thin post / Unclear / Likely scam) with the two or three facts behind it, plus a one-line freshness read (Current / Unclear / Likely stale) with the evidence. Suggest asking the recruiter: "Is this role still open, when was it opened, and where is the process now?" Suggest low-effort ways for the seeker to verify what remains unknown, such as checking the poster's history, matching the role on the company's careers page, or asking for a company email.
