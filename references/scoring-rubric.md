# Scoring rubric (1 to 5)

Score each category on the evidence found. Pair every score with a confidence level: high (multiple reliable sources), medium (one or two sources, or partial), low (sparse or none). Low confidence should be stated openly and pulls the verdict toward "Verify first", not toward a guess.

If the employer is not named or cannot be identified (for example, an agency message), mark the company-dependent categories (stability and growth, team maturity, culture signals) as **Not scored** and say why, instead of guessing.

## Role match
Based only on what the JD and resume state: must-have coverage and the knock-out risks the JD names openly. It does not predict how any company's screening will behave.

5: meets nearly all must-haves, seniority and years in range
4: meets most must-haves, small soft gaps
3: meets the core, with a notable gap (for example, below the asked years but strong adjacent experience)
2: several firm gaps
1: fundamentally different role or level

## Legitimacy and transparency
5: company and poster verified, role on official channels, clear details
4: company verified, poster plausible, post somewhat thin
3: company real, poster or role unverified, many details missing
2: significant inconsistencies
1: probable scam or no identifiable employer

Posting freshness feeds this score: evidence the role is stale or inactive lowers it. An undated post with no other evidence is "unclear" and lowers confidence, not the score.

## Company stability and growth
5: well funded or profitable, growing, clear direction
4: healthy with some uncertainty
3: mixed or limited information
2: signs of instability (layoffs, funding trouble, leadership churn)
1: serious distress

## Team and function maturity
5: established function with senior leadership, defined processes, and room to learn
4: growing function with leadership in place
3: small or new team, ownership high but mentorship unclear
2: function seems an afterthought or poorly supported
1: no evidence of a real team

Note that a new or founding team can suit some seekers (ownership, scope) and not others (mentorship). Weigh against the seeker's stated needs.

## Culture signals
5: consistent positive evidence from reviews and interviews, sizeable sample
4: mostly positive, moderate sample
3: mixed, or too little data (default to 3 with low confidence rather than guessing)
2: recurring concerns
1: serious, consistent concerns

## Alignment with the seeker's constraints
5: satisfies hard filters and supports stated career direction
4: satisfies hard filters, direction partly supported
3: satisfies hard filters, direction neutral
2: one soft constraint broken
1: a hard filter broken

## Verdict mapping (a guide, not a formula)
- Apply: no score below 3 in role match, legitimacy, or constraints, and no severe concern
- Apply with a tailored angle: fit is real but needs framing for a gap or a specific hook
- Verify first: legitimacy, team, or key details are unknown and cheap to check
- Skip: a probable scam, a broken hard filter, or fundamental role mismatch

A single severe issue overrides the rest.
