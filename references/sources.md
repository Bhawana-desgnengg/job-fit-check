# Sources for checking a company, a poster, and a role

Use these in two ways: (1) as Claude's research priority order, and (2) as a short "verify it yourself" list handed to the seeker, so they can cross-check any claim without relying on Claude. Link only to a page Claude actually found in search or fetch results, or to the site's main address below. Do not construct deep links from memory.

## Research priority (most to least reliable)
1. **Primary and official**: the company's own website, careers page and press page; official company registry or stock-exchange filings; regulator sites.
2. **Established news and trade press**: business dailies, sector trade publications, and startup-funding news outlets. Prefer the original report over a rewrite on an aggregator.
3. **Structured company databases**: useful for founding year, funding rounds and headcount ranges, but they can lag or contain errors. Treat as supporting evidence, not proof.
4. **Employee review and salary sites**: useful for patterns, weak for any single company with few reviews. Always state the sample size.
5. **Social posts and forums**: use only as a lead to verify elsewhere, never as the sole basis for a claim.

## Verify it yourself: sample sources by what you want to check

**Is the company real and what is its status?**
- Company registry, by country (use the official government registry, not a paid reseller, where possible):
  - India: MCA (mca.gov.in), company master data
  - United Kingdom: Companies House (find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk)
  - United States: the state Secretary of State business search; SEC EDGAR (sec.gov/edgar) for listed companies
  - Canada: Corporations Canada (ised-isde.canada.ca) and provincial registries
  - Australia: ASIC (asic.gov.au) and ABN Lookup (abr.business.gov.au)
  - Singapore: ACRA (acra.gov.sg)
  - European Union: the e-Justice portal (e-justice.europa.eu) links to national business registers
  - Other countries: search "[country] official company registry". If no reliable free registry exists, say so rather than guess.
- Listed companies: the stock exchange's filings, for example BSE (bseindia.com) and NSE (nseindia.com) in India, NYSE and Nasdaq in the US, the London Stock Exchange in the UK.
- The company's own site and its LinkedIn company page (check that follower count, employee count and posting history look consistent with the claimed size).

**Funding, size and growth**
- Funding and company databases: Crunchbase (crunchbase.com), Tracxn (tracxn.com), CB Insights (cbinsights.com). Cross-check against a news report.
- Business and startup news outlets in the company's country; search the company name with "funding" or "expansion" and read the original article.

**Is the poster or recruiter genuine?**
- Their LinkedIn profile: tenure at the company, history of posts, mutual connections, whether the title makes sense for the company's size.
- The role listed on the company's own careers page, or ask the recruiter to point you to it.
- The email domain: it should match the company's website. Check a domain's age with a WHOIS lookup (who.is or lookup.icann.org).
- Ask for a company email address or a quick video call before sharing documents.

**Team, leadership and culture**
- Team and leadership: the company's About or Team page, LinkedIn "People" tab, and press announcements of senior hires.
- Employee reviews: Glassdoor (glassdoor.com), AmbitionBox (ambitionbox.com), Indeed company pages (indeed.com/companies). Note the number of reviews and their dates.
- Interview experiences: Glassdoor and AmbitionBox interview sections.
- Best source of all: message one or two current or former employees in a similar role and ask three plain questions about the team, the work and the manager.

**Scam and safety checks**
- Search the company name with "scam", "fraud" or "fake job".
- Never pay any fee for a job. See Fraud reporting below.
- Look up older versions of a company page at the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) if a site looks newly built.

## Regional coverage
These sources lean toward India, the UK, the US and a few other English-speaking markets, and the skill works in English. For any other region, say plainly in the answer that registry and review coverage is limited, lower the confidence of the affected scores, and rely more on the company's own site, local news and the poster's profile.

## Fraud reporting
India: cybercrime.gov.in. United States: reportfraud.ftc.gov. United Kingdom: Action Fraud (actionfraud.police.uk). Elsewhere: your national cybercrime or consumer-protection reporting site.

## How to cite for the seeker
In the chat answer, cite each web-sourced claim, and close with a short **"Verify it yourself"** list of 3-5 of the sources above that are most relevant to this opportunity (for example, the registry, the company's careers page, the poster's LinkedIn profile and one review site), with links where you have them. Say which facts rest on one source only.
