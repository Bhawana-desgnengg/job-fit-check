# Worked example 2 (non-design role, different verdict)

An illustrative, fictional composite, not a real company or person. It shows that the format is the same in another field and how the skill behaves when the company is not named. Never reuse these facts.

**Input:** a LinkedIn message from a recruiter at a staffing agency: "Senior Data Analyst, leading logistics client, hybrid, 4-6 years, SQL, Tableau, Python, dbt. Share your resume." No company named. Seeker: data analyst with 3 years of experience, SQL, Tableau and some Python; wants to move toward a team lead role; open to hybrid in their city.

---

**Verdict: Verify first.** The role is a stretch on years and one tool, and the employer is unnamed, so the main unknown is who the client is.

| Category | Score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Role match | 3 | Medium |
| Legitimacy and transparency | 3 | Low |
| Company stability and growth | Not scored | Client not named |
| Team and function maturity | Not scored | Client not named |
| Culture signals | Not scored | Client not named |
| Alignment with your constraints | 4 | Medium |

**Company and legitimacy:** The agency exists and the recruiter's profile shows two years there with a consistent post history (confirmed). The hiring company is not named, so nothing about it can be checked. Naming a client only after you share your resume is common with agencies but means you cannot assess the employer first. **Freshness: Unclear.** The message shows no date and the role was not found on the agency's careers page, so there is no evidence either way on whether it is open.

**Fit with your resume:**
- Must-have coverage: SQL, Covered. Tableau, Covered. Python, Partly (used for data cleaning, not production pipelines). dbt, Missing.
- Stated knock-out risk: 4-6 years asked against 3. The JD does not say whether the range is firm; a screen may treat it as firm.
- Wording gap: the JD says "stakeholder reporting"; the resume describes the same work as "monthly dashboards for the operations team". Using the JD's term is honest.
- Likely probes: ownership of the dashboards, and how the seeker would learn dbt.
- Constraint check: hybrid in the seeker's city is met if the client is local, which is unconfirmed. "Senior" does not clearly move toward a lead role.

**Red flags and unknowns:** Client not named. Pay, location of the client and the interview stages are not stated. Screening tools and cutoffs are not public.

**Questions to ask:** Is this role still open, and when was it opened? Who is the client, and can you share the name before I send my resume? Is the 4-6 years range firm? What are the must-haves versus preferred, the pay range and the interview stages? Is automated screening used?

**Verify it yourself:**
- The agency's website and the role on its careers page (also a check on whether the role is still open).
- The recruiter's LinkedIn profile: tenure, posts, mutual connections.
- The agency's registration in the country's company registry.
- Reviews of the agency on Glassdoor or AmbitionBox, with the number and dates of reviews.
- Searching the agency's name with "scam" or "fake job".

**Outreach:** Reply asking for the client's name, whether the years range is firm, and the pay range, before sending the resume. Offer to share it once those are answered. No resume or ID documents should be sent until the client is named.

End: "Want this as a one-page fit report?"
