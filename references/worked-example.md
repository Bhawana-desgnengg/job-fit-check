# Worked example 1 (design role)

Illustrates the chat output for a hiring post found on LinkedIn. The facts are from one real check in Nov 2026 and are here only to show structure, level of detail and honesty about gaps. Never reuse them for another company, and re-research every time.

**Input:** screenshot of a post by a "Founding Product Designer" at an activewear brand, hiring UI/UX designers with 2-4 years of experience, e-commerce preferred, apply by DM or portfolio in comments. Seeker: product designer with about 1.5 years in design, prior career in teaching, B2B e-commerce and AI search experience, based in the same city.

---

**Verdict: Apply with a tailored angle.** Real, well-funded company and a strong e-commerce match, with a years gap that a screen may treat as firm; the post is thin, so confirm location and team before investing heavily.

| Category | Score | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Role match | 3 | Medium |
| Legitimacy and transparency | 3 | Medium |
| Company stability and growth | 4 | Medium |
| Team and function maturity | 3 | Low |
| Culture signals | 3 | Low |
| Alignment with your constraints | 3 | Medium |

**Company and legitimacy:** Activewear maker with significant outside funding, rapid store expansion and a recently hired head of product and design (cite sources). The poster's profile could not be verified through search. The post gives no location, work mode, pay or JD link, and it has about 95 comments, so a DM will stand out more than a comment. **Freshness: Unclear.** The screenshot shows the post as three days old, but the role was not found on the company's own careers page in search, so whether it is formally open could not be confirmed.

**Fit with your resume:**
- Must-have coverage: UI/UX design and interaction design, Covered. E-commerce (preferred), Covered by B2B e-commerce dashboards, search results layouts and AI search summaries. Visual design, Partly: the resume shows more product and UX work than visual craft, so link a visual sample.
- Stated knock-out risk: 2-4 years asked against about 1.5 years in design. The range reads as soft, but a screen may treat it as a firm filter. A direct DM avoids that route.
- Wording gap: the JD says "interaction design" and "user-centric design"; the resume describes the same work as "user flows" and "conversational UX". Using the JD's terms is honest here.
- Likely probes: depth in visual design, and what the seeker owned versus supported in the e-commerce work.

**Red flags and unknowns:** The poster's profile could not be verified. No culture reviews found, so culture is scored 3 with low confidence. A "founding" title suggests a new or small digital team: ownership likely, mentorship unclear. The company's screening tools, cutoffs and applicant volume are not public.

**Questions to ask:** Is this role still open, and where is the process now? Is this app and web design? Who does the role report to, and how big is the design team? Location and work mode? What are the interview stages, and is automated screening used?

**Verify it yourself:** (3 to 5 sources, linked where found)
- The company's own careers page: check the same role is listed there, which also settles whether it is still open.
- The poster's LinkedIn profile: tenure at the company, post history, mutual connections.
- A funding or news report on the company, cross-checked against a company database such as Crunchbase or Tracxn.
- Employee reviews on Glassdoor or AmbitionBox, noting the number and dates of reviews (here: none found).
- The company registry for the country, to confirm the registered entity.
Note which facts rest on one source only.

**Outreach:** Subject options (pick one, name the role plus the e-commerce match), a four-sentence DM with two concrete matches, portfolio link, the seeker's call offer and one or two questions, and a two-line public comment that moves to DM.

End: "Want this as a one-page fit report?"
